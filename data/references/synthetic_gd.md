This lecture introduces gradient descent as the main optimization algorithm in machine learning. Training minimizes a loss function such as the mean squared error over the model parameters; for convex losses every local minimum is global. The update rule moves the weights against the gradient, scaled by the learning rate. A learning rate that is too large makes training oscillate or diverge, while one that is too small converges slowly; schedules such as step decay or cosine annealing and adaptive methods like Adam and RMSProp help. Stochastic gradient descent estimates the gradient from mini-batches, which is noisy but much cheaper per step. Momentum can accelerate convergence.
